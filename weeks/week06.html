오늘의 큰 그림 · The big idea today

Good news: in the polite 요 form, a statement and a question often look identical — only the intonation changes! 밥을 먹어요. (You eat.) → 밥을 먹어요? (Do you eat?) Just raise your pitch at the end. Question words slot right into the sentence where the answer would go.

💡 격식 있는 질문 · The formal question ending -ㅂ니까/습니까

In very formal settings you may hear -습니까? — like 먹습니까? (Do you eat?) or 갑니까? (Do you go?). You don't need to produce these yet, but it's good to recognise them. For everyday speech, the rising 요 question is perfect!

📌 핵심 · Question words go where the answer goes

Unlike English (which moves the question word to the front), Korean keeps it in the answer's slot:

뭐 먹어요? — What do you eat? (lit. "what eat?")
어디 가요? — Where are you going? (lit. "where go?")
언제 와요? — When are you coming? (lit. "when come?")

💡 발음 팁 · Pronunciation note — 뭐 vs 무엇

무엇 is the full form (more written/formal); 뭐 is the everyday spoken contraction. In conversation you'll almost always say 뭐. "뭐 해요?" = "What are you doing?" — one of the most useful phrases in Korean!

쇼핑 & 개수 · Shopping & quantities
한국어로마자English
이거 얼마예요?igeo eolmayeyo?How much is this?
전부 얼마예요?jeonbu eolmayeyo?How much is it all together?

🛍️ 문화 노트 · Culture note — 깎아 주세요

At traditional markets (시장), it's common to ask for a small discount: "좀 깎아 주세요" (jom kkakka juseyo — "please lower it a little"). Pair it with "이거 얼마예요?" and you're ready to shop like a local! (Note: not used in regular shops or department stores.)
