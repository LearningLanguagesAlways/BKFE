오늘의 큰 그림 · The big idea today

Every Korean verb has a stem (the dictionary form minus 다) and you add a polite ending to it. 먹다 → 먹 + 어요 = 먹어요. Once you know the rule, you can make hundreds of verbs polite — not just the ones you've memorised!

왜 -요? The Magic of -요

-요로 끝나는 말은 정중하면서도 친근해요. 한국 일상의 기본이에요.
Ending a sentence with -요 makes it polite and friendly — the backbone of daily Korean.

함께 Teacher-led

📌 핵심 · The core idea — verbs have a stem

Every verb in the dictionary ends in 다. Remove 다 and you get the stem (어간). You add polite endings to the stem.

Dictionary먹다
→
Stem (remove 다)먹
→
+ polite ending먹어요
같은 동사, 세 가지 단계 · One verb, three formality levels
Formal 합니다체Polite 해요체Casual 반말English

먹습니다먹어요먹어eat
합니다해요해do

💡 왜 해요체를 먼저 배울까요? · Why learn the 요 form first?

It's the level you'll use 90% of the time — with shopkeepers, coworkers, new friends, and teachers. It's polite enough to never offend, and warm enough to feel natural. Master this and you can hold real conversations today!

3

규칙 The -아요 / -어요 Rule

어떤 끝을 붙일지 마지막 모음이 결정해요.
The verb stem's last vowel decides which ending you add. Three simple cases.

함께 Teacher-led혼자 Independent
-아요
If the last stem vowel is ㅏ or ㅗ
살다 → 살아요  ·  좋다 → 좋아요
-어요
All other vowels (ㅓ, ㅜ, ㅣ, ㅡ…)
먹다 → 먹어요  ·  읽다 → 읽어요
-해요
If the verb ends in 하다
공부하다 → 공부해요  ·  일하다 → 일해요

🎵 기억법 · Memory trick — "bright vowels stay bright"

ㅏ and ㅗ are "bright" vowels (Week 1's vowel cosmology!), so they take the bright ending -아요. Everything else takes -어요. And 하다 is special — it always becomes 해요.

소리 합치기 · When vowels merge

When a stem already ends in a vowel, the ending often merges for smoother sound:

DictionaryStem + endingBecomesEnglish
가다가 + 아요가요go
오다오 + 아요와요come
마시다마시 + 어요마셔요drink
주다주 + 어요줘요give
보다보 + 아요봐요see / watch

💡 걱정 마세요 · Don't worry about perfection

The merges feel tricky at first, but your ear learns them fast because you hear them constantly. Focus on the three main rules — the merges will click with practice and listening. Tap the 🔊 buttons and copy the sounds!

불규칙 A Few Friendly Irregulars

몇몇 동사는 살짝 달라요. 자주 쓰는 것만 익혀요.
A handful of verbs change a little. Let's learn just the common, useful ones — no overload!

함께 Teacher-led
ㅡ 탈락 · The ㅡ drops out
DictionaryBecomesEnglish
바쁘다바빠요to be busy
예쁘다예뻐요to be pretty
쓰다써요to write / use
ㄷ → ㄹ & other common ones
DictionaryBecomesEnglish
듣다들어요to listen / hear
걷다걸어요to walk
살다살아요to live
놀다놀아요to play / hang out

⚠️ 지금은 외우지 마세요 · Don't memorise these all at once

Irregulars are best learned one at a time, as you meet them in real sentences — not as a list to cram. For now, just recognise that 듣다 → 들어요 and 바쁘다 → 바빠요. They'll become automatic through use. The regular rule covers the vast majority of verbs!

🌟 가장 중요한 것 · The one to really know

있다 → 있어요 (to exist / have) and 없다 → 없어요 (to not exist / not have). You'll use these every single day — "시간 있어요?" (Do you have time?), "괜찮아요" and so on. We explore them fully in Week 8!

대화 Dialogue & Role-play

진짜 대화예요. -아요/-어요 동사가 가득해요. 소리 내어 읽고 역할을 바꿔요!

🎭 모델 대화 · Daily routines

함께 Read A & B aloud — listen for the 요 verbs!
A · 나
민지 씨, 보통 아침에 뭐 해요?
Minji ssi, botong achime mwo haeyo?
Minji, what do you usually do in the morning?
B · 민지
저는 커피를 마셔요. 그리고 운동해요.
jeoneun keopireul masyeoyo. geurigo undonghaeyo.
I drink coffee. And I exercise.
A · 나
와, 매일 운동해요?
wa, maeil undonghaeyo?
Wow, do you exercise every day?
B · 민지
네, 자주 해요. 알렉스 씨는요?
ne, jaju haeyo. Alex ssineunyo?
Yes, I do it often. How about you, Alex?
A · 나
저는 보통 책을 읽어요. 가끔 영화도 봐요.
jeoneun botong chaegeul ilgeoyo. gakkeum yeonghwado bwayo.
I usually read books. Sometimes I watch movies too.
B · 민지
좋아요! 그럼 같이 영화 봐요.
joayo! geureom gachi yeonghwa bwayo.
Nice! Then let's watch a movie together.
