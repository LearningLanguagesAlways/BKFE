오늘의 큰 그림 · The big idea today

English uses word order to show who does what. Korean uses particles — tiny tags glued to the end of a word. 저는 커피를 마셔요 = "As for me, (I) drink coffee." Once you can spot these markers, you can read any Korean sentence's structure at a glance.

조사 What Are Particles?

조사는 단어 뒤에 붙어서 그 단어의 역할을 알려줘요.
Particles attach to the end of a word to show its job in the sentence.

함께 Teacher-led
은 / 는
Topic marker
"As for…" — sets up what you're talking about. 은 after consonant, 는 after vowel.
이 / 가
Subject marker
Marks who/what does the action. 이 after consonant, 가 after vowel.
을 / 를
Object marker
Marks what the action is done to. 을 after consonant, 를 after vowel.

한 문장에서 모두 보기 · All three in one sentence

저는 커피를 좋아해요

는 = topic (me) 를 = object (coffee)

"As for me, (I) like coffee."

💡 핵심 패턴 · The pattern to remember

Every particle has two forms — one for words ending in a consonant, one for words ending in a vowel. The pattern is the same idea you learned for 이에요/예요 in Week 1. Your ear will start to choose the right one automatically with practice!

🌏 왜 조사가 좋을까요? · Why particles are wonderful

Because particles mark each word's job, Korean word order is flexible and the subject can even be dropped. The particle — not the position — carries the meaning. That's why "커피를 저는 좋아해요" is still understandable: 를 still marks coffee as the object!

은 / 는 Topic Marker

"~로 말하자면" — 주제를 정해 주는 조사예요.
The topic marker means "as for…" — it sets up what the sentence is about.

Ends in a consonant

+ 은

이름 → 이름은  ·  선생님 → 선생님은

+ 는

💡 은/는의 두 번째 역할 · The "contrast" feel

은/는 also gently signals contrast: "커피는 좋아해요" can hint "I like coffee (but maybe not tea)." You don't need to master this yet — just know that 은/는 highlights and compares topics. It's one reason Korean feels so nuanced!

한국어로마자English

🤔 은/는 vs 이/가 — the famous beginner puzzle

Both can look like the English subject, but the feel differs:

• 은/는 (topic): general statements, comparisons, "as for X…".   저는 학생이에요. (Introducing yourself.)
• 이/가 (subject): new information, answering "who?/what?", emphasis on the doer.   누가 와요? — 친구가 와요. ("Who is coming?" — "A friend is.")

팁 · Don't stress about perfection. Native speakers feel this over time — for now, use 은/는 for "talking about" and 이/가 for "pointing out / new info."

을 / 를 Object Marker

행동이 가닿는 대상을 표시해요 — "무엇을?"
The object marker shows what the action is done to — answering "what?".

Ends in a consonant

+ 을

밥 → 밥을  ·  책 → 책을

Ends in a vowel

+ 를

커피 → 커피를  ·  영화 → 영화를

을/를 in action — note the S·O·V order
한국어로마자English

저는 한국어를 공부해요jeoneun hangugeoreul gongbuhaeyoI study Korean

전체 그림 · The whole picture, color-coded

친구가 영화를 봐요

가 = subject (friend) 를 = object (movie)

"A friend watches a movie." (friend · movie · watches)

💬 실생활 팁 · Real-life tip — particles get dropped in speech

In casual conversation Koreans often drop 을/를 (and sometimes 이/가): "밥 먹어요?" instead of "밥을 먹어요?". For writing and clear speech, though, use them — they make your meaning unmistakable. Learn them fully now; drop them later by choice!
