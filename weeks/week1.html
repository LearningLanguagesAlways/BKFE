한국어 소개
Introduction to Korean

Welcome to Week 1! Today you will learn the Korean alphabet — called 한글 (Hangul) — one of the most logical and beautiful writing systems in the world. Once you know the letters, you can read any Korean word aloud!

📚 Did you know?
Hangul was invented in 1443 by King Sejong the Great (세종대왕). He designed it so that ordinary people could read and write — and it worked! Hangul has only 24 letters (14 consonants + 10 vowels), yet it can represent every sound in Korean. Many linguists call it the most scientific alphabet ever created.

Unlike Chinese characters (which number in the thousands), or even the English alphabet (which has tricky silent letters), Hangul is perfectly phonetic: each letter always makes the same sound. If you learn the letters, you can read everything.

🎯 Today's Goals: Learn 10 vowels + 14 consonants · Understand how syllable blocks are formed · Read and write your first Korean words · Begin a simple self-introduction

Vowels — The Basic Ten

Korean vowels are the backbone of every syllable. Every Korean syllable block must contain exactly one vowel. There are 10 basic vowels — think of them as forming two groups: vertical (stand up tall) and horizontal (lie flat).

Tip: Vertical vowels (ㅏ ㅑ ㅓ ㅕ ㅣ) appear to the right of the consonant. Horizontal vowels (ㅗ ㅛ ㅜ ㅠ ㅡ) appear below the consonant.

Building Syllable Blocks

Korean letters are never written alone in a line like English. Instead, they are grouped into square syllable blocks. Each block has a consonant + vowel (and sometimes a final consonant). This is what makes Korean look so distinctive!

ㄱ
ㅏ
가
ㄱ + ㅏ = 가 (ga)
ㄴ
ㅏ
나
ㄴ + ㅏ = 나 (na)
ㅁ
ㅏ
마
ㅁ + ㅏ = 마 (ma)
ㅇ
ㅏ
아
ㅇ + ㅏ = 아 (a)
🔑 The Rule: When a syllable starts with a vowel sound, the consonant ㅇ acts as a silent placeholder. So the vowel ㅏ alone is written as 아 (ㅇ + ㅏ). ㅇ = silent at the start!

자기 소개 연습
Introduction Practice

You now know enough Korean to introduce yourself! Let's practice a simple self-introduction called a 자기소개 (jagi sogae). In Korean, sentences often end with the verb — so "I am a student" becomes something like "I [topic] student [am]."

Teacher-Led Dialogue · 선생님 주도 대화

Practice this dialogue with the class. Teacher speaks as A, students as B. Then switch!

👩‍🏫
안녕하세요! 저는 김 선생님입니다.
Annyeonghaseyo! Jeoneun Kim seonsaengnimimnida.
Hello! I am Teacher Kim.
🙋
안녕하세요! 저는 ___ 입니다.
Annyeonghaseyo! Jeoneun ___ imnida.
Hello! I am ___. (say your name)
👩‍🏫
만나서 반갑습니다!
Mannaseo bangapseumnida!
Nice to meet you!
🙋
저도 반갑습니다!
Jeodo bangapseumnida!
Nice to meet you too!
