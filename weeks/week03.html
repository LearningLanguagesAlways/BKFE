🌏 문화 노트 · Culture note — 잘 먹겠습니다

Before a meal, Koreans often say 잘 먹겠습니다 ("I will eat well") to thank whoever prepared or paid for the food. After eating, 잘 먹었습니다 shows gratitude. It's a small phrase that means a lot — try it at your next Korean meal! 🍚

높임말 Levels of Formality

한국어는 누구에게 말하느냐에 따라 말투가 달라져요. 세 가지 단계를 배워요.
In Korean, how you say something changes with who you're speaking to. Let's meet the three main levels.

함께 Teacher-led
합니다체
Formal 안녕하십니까? 만나서 반갑습니다. annyeonghasimnikka? — business, news, first meetings, elders
해요체
Polite 안녕하세요? 잘 지내요. annyeonghaseyo? — everyday polite: shops, coworkers, new friends
반말
Casual 안녕? 잘 지내? annyeong? — close friends, younger people, family your age or below

🎯 초보자를 위한 황금 규칙 · Golden rule for beginners

When in doubt, use 해요체 (the 요 form). It's polite, friendly, and correct in almost every real-life situation — shops, restaurants, meeting new people. You rarely go wrong with 요!

⚠️ 조심 · Watch out — don't use 반말 too early

Using casual speech (반말) with someone you just met — or someone older — can feel rude, even if you mean well. Wait until a Korean friend says "말 놓으세요" ("let's speak casually") before switching. Politeness is always the safe choice.

어순 Word Order: Subject · Object · Verb

한국어 문장에서 동사는 항상 맨 끝에 와요. 이게 가장 큰 차이예요!
In Korean, the verb always comes last. This is the single biggest difference from English.

함께 Teacher-led혼자 Independent

🇺🇸 English

Subject → Verb → Object

I  eat  rice.

🇰🇷 Korean

Subject → Object → Verb

저는 밥을  먹어요.

문장 뜯어보기 · The sentence, broken down

Subject 주어
저는
I (topic)
→
Object 목적어
밥을
rice (object)
→
Verb 동사
먹어요
eat

더 많은 예시 · More examples — notice the verb is always last

저는 물을 마셔요. — I drink water. (lit. I water drink)
저는 한국어를 공부해요. — I study Korean. (lit. I Korean study)
친구가 책을 읽어요. — A friend reads a book. (lit. friend book reads)

💡 주어 생략 · Koreans often drop the subject

If it's clear from context who you mean, Korean simply leaves the subject out. "밥을 먹어요" can mean "I eat / he eats / we eat" depending on the situation. This feels strange to English speakers at first — but it's everywhere in real Korean!

✏️ 어순 연습 · Rearrange into Korean order

은 / 는 Your First Particle: the Topic Marker

한국어에는 작은 표시(조사)가 있어요. 오늘은 "주제"를 나타내는 은/는을 배워요.
Korean attaches little markers (particles) to words. Today: 은/는, which marks the topic — "as for…".

함께 Teacher-led

📌 규칙 · The rule — it depends on the last sound

Word ends in a consonant → add 은   (예: 이름 → 이름은)
Word ends in a vowel → add 는   (예: 저 → 저는)

Ends in vowel
저 + 는
저는 = "as for me…"
Ends in consonant
이름 + 은
이름은 = "as for the name…"
은/는 in action
한국어로마자English
저는 학생이에요jeoneun haksaeng-ieyoI am a student (as for me)
이름은 민수예요ireumeun Minsu-yeyo(My) name is Minsu
오늘은 월요일이에요oneureun woryoil-ieyoToday is Monday
한국어는 재미있어요hangugeoneun jaemiisseoyoKorean is fun

💡 왜 "주제"일까요? · Why "topic"?

은/는 sets up what you're talking about — like saying "Speaking of me…" or "As for today…". It often lines up with the English subject, so for now you can think of it as a gentle "the subject is…" marker. We'll explore 이/가 (the subject marker) next week!
